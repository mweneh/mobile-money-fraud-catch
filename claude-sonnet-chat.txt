The Proposed Algorithm: SMOTE-Aware Adaptive Boosting (SAAB)

The idea is to build a hybrid that learns when to trust each model based on local class density, rather than applying SMOTE globally or picking one model.
Core Innovation: Three new computational steps

Step 1 — Local Density Scoring (LDS) Instead of global SMOTE, compute a minority density score per sample using k-NN distances. High LDS = data-sparse region (where SMOTE would normally help).

Step 2 — Conditional Synthetic Augmentation (CSA) Apply SMOTE only to high-LDS regions (sparse minority clusters), not globally. This avoids introducing noise in already-dense minority zones.

Step 3 — Adaptive Ensemble Weighting (AEW) Train both LGBM and XGBoost — LGBM on the original distribution, XGBoost on CSA-augmented data. At prediction time, weight their outputs dynamically using the LDS score of the incoming sample:

P_final = (1 - LDS_normalized) × P_LGBM + LDS_normalized × P_XGBoost

High-LDS (sparse) samples lean on XGBoost (SMOTE-trained). Low-LDS (dense) samples lean on LGBM (real-data-trained). This directly operationalizes your empirical finding.

The algorithm: SAAB (SMOTE-Aware Adaptive Boosting)

Your original paper's finding is the key insight: LGBM handles dense, real distributions better, while XGBoost benefits from synthetic augmentation in sparse regions. SAAB operationalizes this directly instead of making a binary model choice.

The three novel steps are:

Step 1 — Local Density Scoring (LDS). For each minority sample, compute a density score using k-NN distances to other minority samples. A high LDS means the sample sits in a sparse neighborhood — exactly where SMOTE tends to help most and where LGBM is flying blind.

Step 2 — Conditional Synthetic Augmentation (CSA). Rather than global SMOTE (which introduces noise in already-dense fraud clusters), augmentation is applied only in high-LDS regions. This is the critical departure from standard pipelines — you're doing targeted resampling, not carpet-bombing.

Step 3 — Adaptive Ensemble Weighting (AEW). At inference time, the final fraud probability is a density-weighted blend: P = (1 − LDS) × P_LGBM + LDS × P_XGBoost. Dense-region transactions trust LGBM. Sparse-region transactions lean on XGBoost trained on augmented data. The switch is continuous, not a hard boundary.
